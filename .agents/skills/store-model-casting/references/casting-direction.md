# Plan faces, then photograph them

A casting brief has two parts: a shared photographic treatment that makes comparison fair, and a specific person whose features differ materially from the rest of the batch.

## Shared direction

Use the requested brand's audience and photographic references. If natural editorial photography is the aim, modest room light, readable skin texture, slight asymmetry and an ordinary stance usually reveal a person better than glamour lighting. A plain garment keeps wardrobe from deciding the casting. Choose framing that leaves the face and visible identity marks inspectable; a complete head and upper body are often useful. Do not force every candidate into an identical expression.

Build the requested batch plan with stable IDs, intended traits, a brief pose/expression and full prompt. Avoid generating extra candidates merely to satisfy an example count in documentation.

## Distinguish candidates

Vary facial length/width, brow, eye shape, nose, jaw, cheeks, hairline and visible marks along with age and proportions. A broad range need not mean every trait is exaggerated. Ordinary waists, posture and small asymmetries help people feel individual. Use the owner's age/body/style direction without inventing a universal definition of who belongs to the brand.

Distribute optional styling deliberately. Tattoos, piercings and unusual hair colors may be useful, but should not all land on every person. Do not change an established person's styling merely because another candidate has it.

An authoring outline, to replace with specific choices:

> Create one original casting photograph of one fictional adult for the requested brand. The person has [concrete facial structure, apparent age, skin/hair and proportions]. Visible marks and styling: [specific choices or none]. Their expression and posture are [a modest motivated choice]. Use [shared framing, wardrobe, light and focus]. Keep natural skin and anatomical proportions. This is a new identity, not a variation of an existing cast member. One continuous photograph with no labels or collage.

This outline is not a final generation prompt. Supply the actual choices and inspect the output; the generator may not follow every trait.

## Review the batch

Use a contact sheet to find repeated faces, not to approve tiny faces. Open likely duplicates and questionable anatomy at native size. Compare candidate faces without allowing hair color or accessories to dominate the judgment. Retain a short reason when replacing a duplicate or anatomy failure, and keep the replacement attached to the requested slot.

A shortlist should name exact stable IDs and anchor files. Browser-local favorites are not a durable selection record until captured. Preserve the selection explicitly so a future agent does not have to reconstruct it from gallery order.
