# Garment-only catalog backgrounds

Use this workflow for product photos with the `garment` role: the shirt alone, with no model. Studio/lifestyle model photos and artwork-only slides keep their separate direction. Apply the caller's brand brief; unless it specifies another treatment, use a warm, light neutral matte surface with subtle texture, soft directional light and natural contact shadows. Oatmeal, light greige or warm gray are useful starting points. Keep a consistent treatment across the collection, with enough contrast for every garment color. Avoid distracting props, strong gradients, heavy texture or an unexplained floating-shirt shadow.

## Preserve the product while changing its surroundings

Inspect the exact current provider garment/color and the approved photo being replaced. Preserve silhouette, neckline, sleeve and bottom construction, fabric color/heathering, artwork, scale and collar clearance. A background change is not permission to alter the shirt or ink.

For a background-only edit, retain the full-resolution approved garment-and-ink composite when its exact-art provenance and placement have been verified. Do not regenerate the shirt or reapply already-correct ink. A provider preview or thumbnail alone does not establish that provenance. If the print needs rebuilding, follow the main skill's original high-resolution artwork and measured-placement workflow first.

Generate or edit the empty background with the host's supported image tool. Composite only when the host permits it and any required user authorization is already present. A style reference supplies lighting, surface and mood; do not copy its garment, artwork or website interface. Match the background light and contact shadow to the preserved garment's existing light. Preserve framing for a background-only replacement.

## Separate cloth edges from the old background and shadow

Keep the garment matte and its contact shadow distinct. A single brightness threshold can mistake neutral shadows for opaque cloth or erase pale, low-saturation fabric. Use an edge treatment suited to the actual source; preserve cloth texture and opaque ink while removing white contamination around the silhouette. Check sleeve/body junctions, collar openings, underarms and hems for trapped wedges of the old background.

Do not reuse another color's mask merely because it is the same garment model. Reuse is appropriate only after verifying that both provider images have the same registered silhouette, crop and pixel coordinates. Review the resulting edge on the receiving color too. Avoid pale/gray fringes, halos, jagged cuts, clipped fabric and invented stitching or fraying.

## Review and replace the selected photo

Compare source and final at full resolution and normal phone size. Inspect difficult edges enlarged as needed, including light-colored garments. Recheck centerline, collar clearance, scale and occlusion, plus fabric color, print fidelity and coherent contact shadows. Unchanged interior pixels can support preservation claims but cannot replace visual review. Keep the original source, background/prompt, matte method, final files and hashes; every image edit invalidates the earlier final-file approval. Use independent review when required by the caller's workflow.

For a request covering all garment-role photos, inventory the actual saved selections across products and colors, including hidden products, and report the replacement count. Do not silently create selections for products without that role. Use the store's supported gallery editor to replace each old selection in its existing slot; preserve descriptions, role, matching view, cover settings and unrelated photos. Removing a selection does not require permanently deleting its recoverable source/history.

Verify the saved choices and loaded images for every replacement. A successful upload alone is not a saved selection. Save privately when the request is for admin drafts; activate or publish only within the user's explicit authorization. Keep generation, review, private delivery and storefront publication distinct.
