Use this reference when the caller wants natural apparel photography with occasional tighter framing. It is an option within a varied set, not a requirement to crop every model's head. The caller's brand direction still controls the cast, activities and styling.

## Apply the framing deliberately

- Mix some clothing-focused close views with face-visible and wider lifestyle exposures. No fixed quota, mandatory head crop or restriction to male models.
- For a close garment view, framing from part of the face through the hips or upper thighs can give the shirt more space. Keep enough collar, shoulders, sleeves and hem to understand fit and drape. Let the scene decide the exact boundary; a crop that looks accidental at phone size needs adjustment.
- Keep a nearby surface, body support or gesture that explains the moment. A tighter picture can still feel outdoors or in a lived-in room. It need not become a floating torso against blurred scenery.
- Preserve uneven, coherent scene light and fabric texture. Do not fill every shadow, add an unexplained rim light or invent texture to make the shirt stand out. Props are optional and subject to the caller's exclusions.
- Move the camera closer or crop the complete photograph to emphasize clothing; never enlarge, raise or recenter the design independently. Preserve provider placement, shirt construction and real occlusion. If compositing after a crop, measure landmarks, projection, folds and masks in that final coordinate space; if cropping a completed composite, transform them all together and rerun final review. Cropping must not hide a failed placement check.
- Keep the original identity anchor and useful wider source framing where available. A head-cropped final is neither a new identity reference nor evidence that likeness passed. Review the visible body, hair and marks against the anchor, and check both the unmarked full image and intended mobile presentation.

## Example brief fragment

Adapt this to the actual garment and selected person; it is not a whole-set template:

> A quiet pause beside a weathered doorway, weight resting naturally against the jamb. Photograph from nearby, with the upper face outside the top edge and the frame continuing through the upper thighs. Keep the collar, sleeves, hem and relaxed hands readable. Daylight from the opening lights one side; the doorway and body contact stay darker. Preserve the selected person's visible identity traits and the exact referenced garment, blank for later artwork placement. No prop is needed. Compose another exposure more widely so the set also includes the person's face and surroundings.
