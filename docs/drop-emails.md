# Drop emails

Optional and off by default. When enabled, everyone on the [design-drop list](drop-signup.md) gets one email about each Design of the Week, on the day it drops. The signup form's own words are the promise, so the email announces the drop and links to the shop, and that is all it may ever do. It sends from the store's own list through the same [Resend](https://resend.com) account as the [owner shipping alerts](order-shipping-alerts.md); no second copy of the list lives at the provider. A newsletter it is not; a polite doorbell it is.

## Behavior

- Once explicitly configured, the example Vercel schedule calls `/api/internal/drop-emails` every 15 minutes (5, 20, 35 and 50 past the hour, clear of the owner alerts on the hour). The route requires `CRON_SECRET`, `DROP_EMAILS_ENABLED=true` and `VERCEL_ENV=production`, so previews and disabled installations cannot send.
- Sending also waits for the store: `configured`, `policies.published` and `features.designOfTheWeek` must be true, and so must `features.dropSignup` or, for a closed list that kept its subscribers, `privacy.retainedDropSubscribers`. The Privacy page describes these emails under the same conditions.
- A drop is a week on the Shop lineup. The first run at or after 9 AM in `storeConfig.timeZone` on the drop's own day freezes that week's email, or at the drop itself when `weeklyFeature` goes live after 9 AM. It is rendered with [React Email](https://react.email) from the live shop as the home page shows it: the week's products, in shop order, leaving out anything nobody can buy right now. A week with nothing scheduled, or nothing for sale, sends nothing.
- Recipients are decided in the same statement: everyone who was signed up when the drop went live. Someone who signs up after the reveal has already seen the drop on the home page. A returning subscriber counts from when they came back, not from their first signup, so the cutoff is the same for them. Because submitting the form again also refreshes that time, someone already back who submits it again after the reveal skips that one drop.
- The window closes at midnight store time. A drop whose day passes without an email stays unannounced, and a first attempt still queued at midnight expires, whether a rate limit, sending switched off or a long queue held it up. So switching sending on mid-week never mails anyone about the drop that already happened. Only an unconfirmed send may be retried after midnight, under its original key.
- Each subscriber gets their own message: one recipient, `From` with `storeConfig.name` on `DROP_EMAIL_FROM_EMAIL`, `Reply-To` the store's `supportEmail`, their own unsubscribe link, and `List-Unsubscribe` plus `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers so Gmail's and Apple Mail's unsubscribe buttons work in one click ([RFC 8058](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8058)). The email uses the store's name, tagline, theme colors and font stacks.
- Product links carry `utm_source=drop-email`, `utm_medium=email` and `utm_campaign=dotw-<date>`, which [order attribution](order-attribution.md) records, so an order that started from a drop email says so on the Orders page. The email has no tracking pixel of its own. Keep Resend's open and click tracking off for the sending domain; the Privacy page mentions neither.

## Unsubscribing

The link in the email opens `/unsubscribe/<id>`, which asks before doing anything, and says so when the list already shows that person as gone. Mail security scanners open every link in a message, and a link that unsubscribed by itself would unsubscribe people nobody asked. The button, and a mail app's one-click POST, both go to `/api/drop-unsubscribe/<id>`. It has no origin check, because a one-click request comes from the mail provider's servers with no Origin at all; the subscriber id in the path is the whole credential, and a GET there does nothing.

The id is the subscriber row's random UUID, never the address. An unsubscribe sets `unsubscribed_at` and keeps the row, as the Privacy page promises, and a second click changes nothing. A later signup puts the address back on the list. `/unsubscribe` is on the private-path list, so no analytics tag ever loads there and sees an id. The support removal procedure in [drop signup operations](drop-signup.md#process-a-removal-request) keeps working alongside the link. To erase someone entirely, delete their `commerce.drop_subscribers` row with the owner's database access; their delivery records go with it.

## Delivery and retries

[`20260930172137_drop_email_deliveries.sql`](../supabase/migrations/20260930172137_drop_email_deliveries.sql) adds two tables. `commerce.drop_emails` holds each drop's frozen subject, HTML, plain text and sender. `commerce.drop_email_deliveries` holds one row per recipient with its status, Resend receipt and attempt time, and no address. Both have RLS and no browser-role privileges; the restricted `store_app_runtime` role can select and insert, and update deliveries.

Each recipient is claimed just before their send, against their subscription as it stands at that moment, first attempts and retries alike. Someone who unsubscribes while a run is sending to others is not written to; only a request already on its way to Resend when they click cannot be called back. The claim marks the send as attempted before Resend is called, under a key made of the drop and the subscriber id. A lost response is retried with that key and the same frozen body for at most 23 hours, inside [Resend's 24-hour deduplication window](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys), then marked expired rather than risk a second copy. A rate limit, a bad key or an unverified sender puts a first attempt back in the queue and stops the run. Three refusals in one run also stop it, because that looks like the sender rather than three bad addresses. When a new week begins, anything still unsent from the last one is marked expired.

Each run sends at most 100 emails, paced at under two a second inside a 45-second budget: roughly 200 an hour. Any error answers the cron with a 503, so keep Vercel cron failure monitoring on. Resend acceptance is **not proof of inbox delivery**; look up the receipt in Resend when someone says an email never arrived. Past a few thousand subscribers, move to Resend's batch endpoint before a drop's queue outlasts its day. Resend's plan limits, including the free plan's daily cap, count these emails too.

## Admin

**Products → Drop emails** (`/admin/products/emails`) shows whether sending is on, and names any missing setting; how many people are subscribed and unsubscribed, never who; a sandboxed preview of the next drop's email, built from its products even while they are hidden; a **Send a test** button that sends that preview to the store's `supportEmail` only, marked `[Test]`, with an unsubscribe link that matches nobody; and the last twelve drop emails with how many went out. A test waits for `storeConfig.configured`, because an unconfigured store's support address is still the template placeholder.

## Rollout

The template has no active schedules. Review hosting-plan support and merge the drop email entry from `scripts/vercel-crons.example.json` into `vercel.json` only for an authorized setup.

1. Apply `supabase/migrations/20260930172137_drop_email_deliveries.sql`. It is additive: an app without drop emails never reads it, and a deploy without it only makes the Drop emails page and the cron report that the tables are unavailable.
2. Configure production-only `RESEND_API_KEY` with sending access to a verified domain, and set `DROP_EMAIL_FROM_EMAIL` to an address on that domain, such as `drops@` the store's domain. Mail goes out with the store's name, and replies go to its support address. Set values directly in deployment secrets, never in notes, chat or source.
3. Set `STORE_POSTAL_ADDRESS` to the store's mailing address, on one line. US law ([CAN-SPAM](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)) requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email; a P.O. box or a registered private mailbox qualifies. Sending refuses to start without it. Check the rules for every place the store sells to; this template does not decide them.
4. Review the Privacy page's design-drop section with the owner, including the provider and delivery-record disclosure that appears once sending is on. Deploy, open **Drop emails**, check the preview, and send a test. Confirm it reaches the support inbox with working product links, and that the original message carries the `List-Unsubscribe` headers (Gmail: **Show original**).
5. Set `DROP_EMAILS_ENABLED=true` and redeploy before the next drop. `pnpm setup:check --launch` then checks the sender, address, store switches and schedule; it cannot verify delivery. Check that day's run in the Drop emails history and its receipts in Resend.
6. Roll back sending with `DROP_EMAILS_ENABLED=false`. The outbox, and the unsubscribe links in emails already sent, keep working. Delivery records remain until the owner's retention process removes them, so keep the Privacy page accurate for them.

## Validation

`DROP_EMAIL_TEST_DATABASE_URL` and `DROP_EMAIL_OVERVIEW_TEST_DATABASE_URL` must target isolated local `store_drop_email_test` and `store_drop_email_overview_test` databases. Both suites apply every migration and run as `store_app_runtime`. They test who is addressed (returning subscribers through the real signup store), the 9 AM and midnight boundaries for freezing and for first attempts, freezing once, retries under the original key and their 23-hour cutoff, the stop on a rate limit or repeated refusals, unsubscribes between runs and in the middle of one, a new week closing out the last, the run lock, and browser-role denial. The signup store suite covers unsubscribing through the same role, and the public browser suite covers the unsubscribe page, one-click POST and a scanner's GET. Provider tests use fixtures: they do not prove Resend delivery or a production deployment.
