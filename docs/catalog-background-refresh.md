# Background catalog refresh

Shop, product pages, metadata, sitemap, Meta cart resolution, and merchant feeds read our database. They never call the Printify API, including during cold starts, cache misses, missing configuration, or upstream outages. `PRINTIFY_PUBLISHING_ENABLED=true` is required for browsing products and starting checkout; catalog reads stay database-only. Fresh stores populate the catalog through normal signed publication events.

Publishing still controls listing membership, titles, prices, selected variants, photos, and approved artwork. The polling jobs never change those records or acknowledge a publication. Checkout and paid fulfillment keep their separate uncached provider checks. Customer actions that create/edit private Printify drafts also retain their provider operations.

## Jobs and storage

Merge the reviewed schedules from `scripts/vercel-crons.example.json` into `vercel.json` after verifying hosting-plan support and owner authorization. The template has no active schedules. The supported commercial Vercel setup is **Pro or Enterprise**: [current cron limits](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing), checked **2026-09-27**, allow minute-level schedules there; Hobby permits only daily runs and is [restricted to noncommercial use](https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby). A daily stock refresh cannot satisfy this app's two-hour cutoff. Follow [hosting setup](store-setup.md#supported-hosting-and-scheduled-workers), then verify authenticated execution after deployment.

| Job                                     | Example production schedule | Requests                   | Observation freshness |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------- |
| `/api/internal/catalog-sync/stock`      | Every 30 minutes            | Shop products, 50 per page | Less than two hours   |
| `/api/internal/catalog-sync/popularity` | Every six hours             | Shop orders, 10 per page   | Less than 24 hours    |

Stock and popularity observations share Next.js data caches across requests, with five-minute fallback revalidation. Successful jobs invalidate their own observation cache; a recent-job retry also invalidates it to recover a missed invalidation after commit. Warm Shop/product requests need no catalog database reads. The original observation timestamp is checked outside the cache on every request, so cache reuse cannot extend either freshness limit. Checkout always reads current provider stock before creating a new payment session, even when browsing still shows an available option.

After explicit configuration, these schedules allow delayed browsing availability while checkout verifies live stock. The freshness limits allow several missed refreshes before browsing availability or popularity becomes unavailable. With no stock worker, a previously initialized store loses browsing availability at the two-hour observation cutoff, and feeds fail with 503; publishing products does not replace the required complete stock scan. The example publishing recovery worker runs every minute to process queued events; an idle run makes no Printify requests.

Both routes accept GET for Vercel cron and POST for an authenticated manual run. They require `Authorization: Bearer <CRON_SECRET>` before any database or provider access. Both use the existing `PRINTIFY_API_TOKEN`, `PRINTIFY_SHOP_ID`, `DATABASE_URL`, and verified database TLS. The token needs `products.read` and `orders.read`. Publishing and webhook credentials remain separate. Vercel runs schedules only on production deployments; preview verification must invoke the routes directly.

Workers share Printify's account-level rate limits with drafts, publishing, checkout and any other shop on that account. Separate tokens do not add quota. Follow [token expiry/rotation and API limits](store-setup.md#printify-and-the-first-product), and consider aggregate load before increasing cadence or batch size.

[Printify's products endpoint](https://developers.printify.com/#products) returns up to 50 products in one request. The stock job scans the shop with `limit=50`, including pages containing unrelated drafts, then retains only published-product variant IDs, enabled/in-stock flags, and production fingerprints. It never persists draft titles, prices, artwork URLs, or production payloads. Absent products receive an empty stock projection after a complete scan. The scan is limited to 20 pages and 45 seconds, with a fifteen-second per-page request deadline; exceeding a bound is a failed refresh, never a complete snapshot.

Publication also saves the worker's already-read live stock, with its original read timestamp, in the same transaction as the listing revision. New, re-shown, and re-produced products use that stock immediately when it is newer than the complete scan. The next newer scan takes over. This adds no Printify request and does not extend the complete scan's two-hour limit; missing/expired global observations still require a successful refresh. Visibility-only publication preserves saved display fields even when the live draft is malformed; that product's stock is then unavailable.

The popularity job preserves the existing qualifying production/fulfillment statuses and sample/reprint exclusions. It scans at most 200 pages within 30 seconds and saves only per-product unit counts, including products with qualifying sales that are currently hidden. Readers expose only active-product totals; IDs absent from the complete aggregate start at zero until the next scan, keeping Most Popular usable when membership changes. Customer data and raw order records are not persisted.

Each complete observation atomically replaces one `(shop_id, kind)` row in the private `commerce.catalog_refreshes` table. Row-level security and explicit grants restrict access to the application login. Its observation time is captured before scanning. Per-shop/job advisory locks prevent overlapping workers; a successful observation less than 29 minutes old for stock or five hours 59 minutes old for popularity also avoids repeat provider requests. Scheduled GET runs retain one minute of tolerance before the next tick. Authenticated manual POSTs bypass this age guard, including after membership changes or a fingerprint-version deployment. They retain the same advisory lock, scan bounds, validation, and atomic replacement; `busy` means retry after the in-progress job completes. No direct timestamp edits are needed. Stock and popularity have separate locks and can progress independently. Provider I/O reserves worker connections while leaving a database read connection available for page requests.

Failed or incomplete scans leave the last complete observation intact. Stock pagination rejects duplicate identities, inconsistent page/total metadata, and wrong-shop products. A selected product whose production data fails parsing is stored as an empty stock projection with the sanitized `CATALOG_PRODUCT_INVALID` warning, isolating the fault to that product. Storefront availability closes when stock is missing, too old, or from a different fingerprint version; published photos and descriptions remain visible. Feeds return 503 for missing/stale observations rather than replace a destination catalog with misleading empty or sold-out data. An absent entry in a fresh observation omits only that product from feeds. An explicit empty entry means the complete scan found no usable stock and represents that product as unavailable. Popularity becomes unavailable after 24 hours, not when a product joins the catalog.

The authenticated publishing health endpoint reads observation timestamps directly from Postgres. It reports `CATALOG_STOCK_STALE` at 65 minutes and `CATALOG_POPULARITY_STALE` at 13 hours, before the two-hour/24-hour browsing cutoffs. Missing observations use the same warning codes. Invalid payloads/version mismatches report `CATALOG_STOCK_INVALID` or `CATALOG_POPULARITY_INVALID`; storage failures report `CATALOG_REFRESH_UNAVAILABLE`. The existing GitHub publishing monitor recognizes these signals and opens or maintains its assigned incident after two unhealthy probes. Reader expiry also emits sanitized diagnostics. Review these warnings before observations expire; do not reset publication callbacks to repair stock.

## Setup and verification

1. Apply the [fresh database baseline](../supabase/migrations/20260928000354_storefront_baseline.sql) to the new empty store database with [Supabase CLI `db push` and verify migration history](store-setup.md#database-and-tls), then provision the runtime login. Configure the intended shop, tokens, canonical URL and cron secret through [store setup](store-setup.md).
2. Deploy the reviewed configuration within the owner's authorized scope, with publishing enabled only when the complete connection is ready. Configure worker schedules explicitly for the hosting plan.
3. On the intended deployment, send authenticated **POST** requests to `/api/internal/catalog-sync/stock` and `/api/internal/catalog-sync/popularity`. Keep bearer and deployment-protection credentials out of logs. Require `result: "updated"`; `recent`, `busy` and `disabled` do not prove initialization. Retry `busy` after the active scan finishes. Both routes read Printify and save observations; neither publishes a draft.
4. Verify fresh durable timestamps, Shop, a purchasable product, both merchant feeds, Most Popular and authenticated publishing health. An empty new shop can verify the connection but cannot establish a saleable product. Confirm recurring successful invocations after deployment; HTTP 200 from a skipped run is insufficient.
5. In isolated fixtures, test malformed products, provider outage, expired observations and forced recovery. A failed scan preserves the previous observation; a successful later scan restores availability without requiring provider requests during page/feed reads.

Configuration and local tests do not activate remote schedules or prove production permissions. Record actual provider execution separately.

## Local checks

`CATALOG_TEST_DATABASE_URL` must point to an isolated local database named `store_catalog_test`. Its database tests verify real runtime permissions, cross-instance persistence, atomic failure recovery, and exclusion of overlapping jobs. `CATALOG_E2E_DATABASE_URL` must point to an isolated local `store_catalog_e2e`; the public browser suite resets it and seeds published snapshots/observations. Never point either at shared or production data. The enabled-publishing browser suite uses its existing isolated `store_publishing_e2e` and tests real HTTP refreshes plus zero provider calls on cold/repeated page and feed reads. CI provisions all three databases.
